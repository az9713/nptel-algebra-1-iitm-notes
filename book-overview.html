A route through the book

The course moves from mathematical language to the classification of linear operators. Its connecting idea is preservation of structure: a function, a quotient, a field embedding, or a basis change makes sense only after identifying exactly which information it retains.

Three reading routes

The main dependency spine

  1. Chapters1–4: logical statements, proof methods, functions, equivalence classes, and well-defined maps.
  2. Chapters5–8: operations, groups, rings, integer axioms, induction, and finite algebraic manipulations.
  3. Chapters9–14: cardinality, divisibility, quotient arithmetic, fields, ordered completeness, complex numbers, and formal polynomials.
  4. Chapters15–19: elimination, matrices, affine solution sets, vector spaces, bases, exchange, and dimension.
  5. Chapters20–23: linear maps, coordinates, eigenspaces, invariant subspaces, and polynomial identities.
  6. Chapters24–25: invariant flags, triangularization, nilpotent chains, and Jordan classification.

Quotients appear first as a language for identifying equivalent objects, then as a way to build rational numbers and congruence rings, and finally as a tool for reducing operator problems. The same well-definedness check returns each time: changing a representative must not change the result.

How to use the worked problems

Every chapter has six problems with complete solutions. Start with the problem statement closed to its solution. Write the hypotheses, identify the relevant earlier result, and produce a proof or calculation. Then compare the reasoning rather than only the final expression. Where your argument differs, test its boundary cases: empty sets, zero spaces, a noninjective function, a nonzero characteristic, or a polynomial whose roots leave the base field.

The Python listing in each chapter checks a finite instance or exact calculation. It is a laboratory companion to the proof. Run it with the documented dependencies, change an input, and predict which assertion should fail. The algebra laboratory also provides an offline chain explorer.

Conventions that prevent hidden changes of problem

  • Fields have distinct zero and one. Ring and homomorphism conventions are stated in their chapters; an assumption of a multiplicative identity is explicit.
  • Natural-number indexing and empty-sum/product conventions are stated where introduced. Zero vector spaces and empty bases remain part of finite-dimensional theorems.
  • Polynomials are formal coefficient objects. Over finite fields, equal evaluated functions need not be equal polynomials.
  • The scalar field is part of a vector-space problem. Real and complex eigenvalues, and differentiation in positive characteristic, behave differently.
  • Coordinate vectors are columns. The matrix of a map has images of domain basis vectors as columns. Upper Jordan blocks require chains ordered from the killed vector toward the chain generator.
  • “Proved here” refers to the supplied mathematical argument. It is not a claim of proof-assistant certification.

Background assumptions and review limits

The construction of a complete real ordered field and its uniqueness are identified as background existence results in Chapter13. The fundamental theorem of algebra is an explicit background theorem in Chapter14 and later complex operator arguments. Elementary real trigonometric identities are used for rotations. The book does not develop a complete set-theoretic foundation, all infinite-dimensional basis theory, determinants, Galois theory, inner-product spectral theory, or numerical eigenvalue algorithms.

A useful next connection

The final decomposition has a natural continuation in module theory. Let a polynomial act on a vector by $p\cdot v=p(T)v$. Then invariant subspaces are precisely subspaces stable under this polynomial action. The polynomial division and Bézout arguments already proved explain why factorization can separate operator components. A later course can organize these observations using modules over $F[X]$; this book establishes the concrete results without assuming that theory.
